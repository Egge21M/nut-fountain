# Faster camera scanning and phone diagnostics

Replace the fixed post-scan 100 ms pause with fresh-video-frame scheduling. Keep raw binary and UR compatibility, move jsQR work to a worker, transfer pixel buffers, and allow only one frame in flight. Reuse capture canvas dimensions. Prefer 60 FPS camera capture without requiring hardware support; fall back to animation frames with repeated-video-frame suppression. Keep the sender default at 5 FPS and expose rates through 60 FPS for testing.

Expose a local JSON diagnostic download after completion or manual stop, with an optional sender FPS annotation. Include camera/browser/build context, stage timings, attempted scans, QR reads, useful rank changes, busy skips, presentation gaps, and bounded per-second samples. Exclude token data, QR payloads, checksums, camera images, mint URLs, and camera identifiers. Clear reports on reader reset and replace them on a new camera session. Camera sessions that resume an existing reader report only their own rank gains.

Validation completed: production build and TypeScript checking; 10 playground unit tests; 15 Chromium browser checks covering binary and UR recovery, worker decoding, animation-frame fallback, downloadable reports, privacy, bounded storage, cancellation, permission failure, asynchronous worker failure, and resource cleanup. A synthetic 30 FPS blank camera stream produced about 25.5 scan attempts per second on this server; this is not a physical-device or successful-QR throughput measurement. Physical-device rates remain to be measured using exported reports.

## Comments

2026-10-05 — iPhone zero-progress regression: Chrome on iPhone 17 Pro reported 677 video callbacks but only one captured/completed attempt, 675 repeated-frame skips, one busy skip, and no QR reads over 11.355 seconds. The camera and worker were active; the media timestamp used for frame identity remained unchanged while `presentedFrames` advanced. Rejecting repeated `mediaTime` therefore suppressed every image after the first.

Reproduction: feed a moving canvas camera whose video callbacks retain a constant `mediaTime`, with a blank first second followed by QR frames at 5 FPS. The deployed version-2 reader stayed at zero progress (20 callbacks, 1 attempt, 19 repeated skips). The retained browser regression starts the same way and failed waiting for completion before the fix. The targeted replay is `/tmp/nut-fountain-scan-regression/constant-time.ts` (temporary debugging harness).

Correction: use `metadata.presentedFrames` as the video-callback frame identity; retain the existing playback-counter/time fallback only for animation-frame scheduling. The same replay now advances (5 callbacks, 5 attempts, 1 useful equation, no repeated skips). Scanner diagnostics are version 3 to distinguish the corrected build. Keep the constant-timestamp case in `apps/playground/scripts/browser.ts`; desktop mobile viewport emulation alone did not expose this iPhone metadata behavior.

2026-10-05 — Extend the sender slider to 60 FPS (default remains 5). Completed reader results and diagnostic JSON show elapsed time from the first accepted frame through validation, and approximate reconstructed payload throughput in decimal kB/s. Reader timing spans camera pauses and image imports, resets with the reader, and ignores rejected input. Incomplete transfers omit completion metrics; zero-duration transfers omit throughput.

2026-10-05 — Density experiment: user reports a sweet spot near 20 FPS and about 0.3 seconds for the existing 180-byte setting. Add fragment sizes 256, 384, 512, 768, 1024, 1536, 2048, and 2307 bytes, keeping the default 128 and error correction M. The 24-byte header makes 2307 the maximum payload for a version-40 byte-mode QR; 2308 fails the encoder capacity check. Display QR version and module dimensions. Preserve padding when the token is smaller than the selected fragment, so all densities remain testable. Validation: TypeScript/production build, 13 playground tests, and real Chromium local pixel round trips for all 11 picker settings with mobile layout checks. Physical camera scan limits remain device-dependent.

2026-10-05 — Camera selection: user reports iOS switching lenses during scanning. The app previously requested only facingMode=environment. Add a camera picker populated after permission, requesting the selected deviceId with exact matching and no competing facingMode preference. Release the previous camera before opening a replacement, preserve reader progress and the choice across reader resets, reject unavailable selections without automatic fallback, and keep enumeration optional. Device IDs and labels stay in view state only; scanner version 5 reports automatic vs explicit-device selection without identifiers. Individual physical camera selection can avoid virtual-device switching; a Dual/Triple device can still change its constituent lens. No physical iPhone repro is available on this server, so this is a tested selection mechanism, not a verified fix for the device's reported lens behavior.

Evidence: https://bugs.webkit.org/show_bug.cgi?id=253186 distinguishes virtual/physical cameras and describes exact device selection; https://w3c.github.io/mediacapture-main/ specifies camera device constraints; https://raw.githubusercontent.com/webrtc/samples/gh-pages/src/content/devices/input-output/js/main.js demonstrates device enumeration and stopping old capture before selecting a new device. Do not infer physical lens type from localized labels or enumeration order.

Browser feedback loop: `bun /tmp/nut-fountain-camera-choice/check.ts` failed on the old build (camera picker absent), then passed with explicit-device constraints, old-stream cleanup, no automatic reopen loop, retained selection, no fallback after OverconstrainedError, cleanup of cancelled late streams, and continued capture if enumeration fails. This models browser selection, not iOS physical switching. The test is retained as `apps/playground/scripts/camera-selection.ts` and invoked by the browser suite.
